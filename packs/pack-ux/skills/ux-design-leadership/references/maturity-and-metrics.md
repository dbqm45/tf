## 1. The NN/g maturity model
Six stages (accessed 2026-10-17): 1 Absent, 2 Limited (UX on the initiative of individuals,
without roles and budget), 3 Emergent (more work in teams, but unstable and without a shared
strategy), 4 Structured (there are UX teams, a shared prioritization method, often based on
business metrics), 5 Integrated (a lasting practice; according to NN/g the highest stage that
most organizations reach), 6 User-driven (knowledge about users shapes the company's direction
[unverified: the NN/g wording]). Factors: strategy, culture, process, outcomes. The model is not a linear
checklist: an organization has different stages on different factors.

Use: a table `factor | stage | evidence | what next`. At stages 1–2 start with one visible win and
a sponsor; at 3–4 with shared standards and roles; above that with influence on the product strategy.

## 2. HEART
Five categories (Google, Rodden, Hutchinson, Fu, 2010): Happiness (attitudes, e.g. satisfaction), Engagement,
Adoption (new users or features), Retention (returning users), Task success (effectiveness,
efficiency, errors). Process: for each chosen category a **Goal** (what we want), a **Signal** (how it shows
in behaviour or opinion), a **Metric** (the number we track). Practice: no more than ~3 goals per project (a recommendation
of a secondary source). Apply it to a product or a feature.

## 3. SUS and task success
SUS: 10 questions, a score of 0–100; the average from a large MeasuringU data set is 68 and corresponds to about the 50th percentile — the score
is not a "pass" percentage. A target of e.g. 80 is a popular industry target (a secondary source). Task success: the share of
participants completing the task without help; task time; errors. Sample size and the interpretation of confidence:
ux-research-methods and data-analysis. NN/g benchmarks for task success [unverified].
