# Competitive cheat sheet for sales

One page per competitor. The owner and the update date in the header; a review every quarter or after a
major change at the competitor. Sources: win and loss data (CRM, conversations after the customer's decision), the competitor's price list
and documentation with the date read, customer opinions.

## Fields
1. **In brief** — who the competitor is, for whom, the pricing model (2–3 sentences, with a date).
2. **When we win** — the customer type and situation (from data, e.g. "firms with > 150 customers, many banks").
3. **When we lose** — honestly; the salesperson then knows when not to fight.
4. **Our differences** — 3 attributes with value and evidence (from positioning).
5. **Questions for the customer** — questions about the need whose answer itself shows our advantage
   ("How many banks do you reconcile every month?"). No questions that make the competitor look ridiculous.
6. **Objections and answers** — the most common 3–5 charges ("more expensive", "no integration with…"), an answer with evidence.
7. **Evidence** — customer cases, numbers, quotes — with a date and the customer's consent to use.

## Rules
- Only verifiable facts, with a date; an outdated cheat sheet harms more than none.
- No disparagement and no gossip — the salesperson loses the customer's trust, and the material ends up with the competitor.
- External comparisons (a website, a leaflet) are comparative advertising: objective and verifiable attributes (Directive
  2006/114/EC, Art. 4) — a legal review before publication.
- After each quarter: the win rate against this competitor before and after the cheat sheet was introduced.
